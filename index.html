Projects

A selection of engineering projects spanning industry and academic work — each focused on solving a real manufacturing or design challenge.

Automated Solar Busbar Assembly Line

Aptera Motors · Industry

Early solar panel prototypes required days to assemble using soft tooling. I designed and deployed a robotic pick-and-place system using a Fanuc robot arm, integrated with a high-efficiency stringer to automate solar string production. MATLAB modeling was used to optimize robotic movement and sequencing. The result: production time dropped from days to under 10 minutes per panel — roughly a 100× cycle time reduction — with consistent, validated cell placement throughout.

Battery Pack Durability & Potting Process

Aptera Motors · Industry

Battery packs were failing under vibration due to insufficient shock absorption and inconsistent hand-mixed potting foam. I evaluated multiple foam shock absorbers against strict height constraints, identified root causes of uneven curing, and sourced an industrial mixing solution to replace the manual process. I developed a controlled dispensing method and a lid-pressing technique to remove excess material before sealing. The redesigned process passed both vibration and high-impact durability tests, establishing a scalable, repeatable manufacturing baseline.

Remote Center of Motion (RCM) Mechanism

Academic Project · Surgical Robotics

Surgical tools must pivot about a fixed entry point without damaging surrounding tissue — but relying on sensors or software for this constraint introduces failure modes. I designed a double-parallelogram linkage that enforces a remote center of motion purely mechanically, with 2 rotational degrees of freedom and 1 prismatic DOF for tool insertion. I built the full CAD assembly, ran motion analysis to validate trajectory behavior, and characterized singularities and workspace limits. The physical prototype confirmed consistent RCM behavior throughout its hemispherical workspace.

3D-Printed Aircraft — Modular Wing System

Engineering Competition · 2× Award Winner

The team's initial aircraft had a non-uniform wing structure that made field repairs slow and costly. I proposed a modular wing architecture allowing individual sections to be swapped instead of reprinting the entire wing. I also designed an adjustable mechanism to fine-tune the aircraft's center of gravity on-field, and tested lightweight PLA blends alongside reinforced materials at high-stress joints. The redesigned aircraft won the Longest Flight Award for sustained air time and the Most Innovative Design Award — validating rapid iteration through modular design as a competitive strategy.
